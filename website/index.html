Two ways to prepare

Every case starts the same way. From there, choose the mode that fits what you need right now.

  1. 1

    Describe the case

    Dictate or type a real case in your own words — no patient identifiers, just the clinical picture.

  2. 2

    Choose your mode

    Preparing for an actual conference, or want to sharpen your reasoning on a case you saw? Pick Heart Team or What Would You Do.

  3. 3

    Work through it

    Answer a few focused questions or start talking it through — either way, you come away sharper than when you started.

Mode 1

Heart Team

Structured prep for an actual conference presentation.

1

Answer a few focused questions The Copilot asks only what's still missing — nothing you've already told it.

2

Review your nine-section report Diagnosis, indication, missing information, operative strategy, alternatives, controversies, technical considerations, postoperative concerns, and evidence/guidelines with PubMed references.

Mode 2

What Would You Do

A live discussion to pressure-test your reasoning.

1

Get a short presentation and a starting question The Copilot condenses your case and opens with the real decision point.

2

Talk it through with an AI attending Follow-up questions, complicating factors, and pushback on your reasoning — the way a good attending actually teaches.

Built for real conference prep

Structured conference reports

Diagnosis through postoperative concerns, organized the way you'd actually present it — not a wall of text.

Honest about what's missing

A dedicated "missing information" section names the gaps and assumptions you'll want to confirm before you stand up.

Evidence and guidelines

Automatically search PubMed for abstracts relevant to your case's controversies and technical considerations.

Socratic discussion mode

What Would You Do pushes back, asks why, and introduces complicating factors — practice for the real thing.

Built specifically for cardiac surgery

Not a general surgery tool with a cardiac skin — dictation, terminology, and both workflows are built around cardiac surgery cases from the ground up.

  • Cardiac surgery vocabulary & dictation
  • Clinical reasoning practice
  • Evidence & guideline search
